# Customer Psychology & Behavioral Design Skill

This skill governs the intentional application of cognitive psychology and behavioral economics to remove friction, build customer confidence, accelerate comprehension, and foster sustainable operational habits.

**The Ethical Ironclad Rule:**
Psychology must always be used to serve the customer's genuine transformation and long-term interest. The AI Product Factory strictly prohibits manipulative, coercive, or deceptive behavioral patterns.

---

## 1. Ethical Boundary: Absolute Prohibition of Dark Patterns

The following tactics are permanently banned from all products, marketing materials, and checkout flows:
- **Zero Fake Scarcity:** No fabricated "Only 3 copies left" or artificial inventory limits on digital goods.
- **Zero Fake Urgency:** No looping countdown timers, artificial deadline counters, or fake "flash sale" expiration banners.
- **Zero Fabricated Social Proof:** No fake testimonials, non-existent customer quotes, or deceptive "X people are viewing this right now" popups.
- **Zero Deceptive Pricing:** No hidden add-ons, forced auto-renewing subscriptions disguised as one-time purchases, or bait-and-switch pricing tiers.
- **Zero Roaches & Obstacles:** No convoluted multi-step cancellation flows, hidden opt-outs, or intentionally obscured unsubscribe buttons.

---

## 2. Core Psychological Levers for High-Utility Design

### A. Cognitive Load Reduction & Chunking
- Working memory is finite (Miller's Law: $7 \pm 2$ chunks, modern consensus $4 \pm 1$).
- **Technique:** Break complex multi-step workflows into small, digestible phases.
- **Application:** Never present a 40-question intake form on a single screen. Group into logical milestones (Diagnostic $\rightarrow$ Architecture $\rightarrow$ Execution).

### B. Progressive Disclosure
- Show only the information needed for the current step; defer advanced configuration until the user requests it.
- **Application:** In financial models, keep high-level drivers visible on the summary dashboard while isolating granular operational assumptions on dedicated background tabs.

### C. The Goal-Gradient Effect & Visible Progress
- Motivation increases as people perceive themselves getting closer to a goal.
- **Technique:** Provide visible progress bars, checklist tallies (`Step 2 of 5`), and pre-fill the first milestone as completed (e.g. `[x] Account Initialized`) to eliminate the cold-start barrier.

### D. Implementation Intentions & Commitment Devices
- Humans fail to act on vague resolutions ("I will improve team deployments"). They succeed when armed with explicit "If-Then" commitments ("If the PR review queue hits 3 items, then we trigger morning pair triage").
- **Application:** Workbooks and playbooks must include fillable Implementation Contracts with explicit dates, times, triggers, and sign-offs.

### E. Smart Defaults & Choice Architecture
- Decision fatigue leads to drop-off.
- **Technique:** Pre-select sensible, battle-tested industry standard settings. Users should only need to change settings if their domain demands an exception.

### F. Peak-End Rule
- People judge an experience largely by how it felt at its peak (the most intense or satisfying point) and at its end.
- **Technique:** 
  - Engineer a thrilling "First Value" moment within the first 180 seconds.
  - Conclude the product experience with a clear, celebrating milestone (e.g. an exportable executive report, a maturity badge, or a concrete 90-day action roadmap).

### G. Identity-Based Motivation
- People act in alignment with the professional identity they aspire to embody.
- **Technique:** Speak to the customer's desired professional standards (e.g. "How World-Class Reliability Engineers Operate", "The High-Velocity Technical Lead").

### H. Cognitive Ease & Visual Contrast
- Information that is easy to perceive and read is intuitively judged as more truthful and reliable.
- **Technique:** High-contrast typography ($\ge 75\%$ black body text), clean visual rhythm, generous margins, and bold lead-in phrases for list items.

---

## 3. The Psychology Audit Checklist

Before releasing any product or onboarding experience, verify:
- [ ] Is Time to First Value under 180 seconds?
- [ ] Are complex tasks chunked into 3–5 step sequences?
- [ ] Are smart defaults pre-populated so the user is never stranded on a blank canvas?
- [ ] Does the product feature clear progress visibility?
- [ ] Are implementation intentions explicitly designed for key actions?
- [ ] Is every claim, testimonial, and social proof metric 100% verified in `memory/sources.csv`?
- [ ] Are all dark patterns completely absent?
