# OPPORTUNITY SCORING SYSTEM & RUBRIC

Scores are decision-support estimates designed to prioritize hypotheses systematically. They are **not** objective mathematical certainty. Every score must be substantiated with verifiable evidence recorded in `memory/sources.csv`.

---

## 1. Core Dimensions & Scoring Rubrics (0–100)

### 1. Demand (Weight: 0.12)
Measures the volume, persistence, and breadth of observable problem signals across target communities.
- **0–25 (Negligible):** Isolated mentions; zero repeated patterns across independent threads.
- **26–60 (Emerging):** Occasional discussions in niche forums; sporadic mentions without high community engagement.
- **61–85 (Strong):** Frequent recurring threads, upvoted complaints, and active discussions across multiple independent communities.
- **86–100 (Exceptional):** Ubiquitous, persistent bottleneck affecting widespread users across multiple platforms over extended time windows.

### 2. Pain Intensity (Weight: 0.12)
Measures the acute cost, emotional frustration, operational risk, or wasted time caused by the unresolved problem.
- **0–25 (Minor annoyance):** Slight inconvenience; users easily shrug it off.
- **26–60 (Moderate friction):** Noticeable slowdown or annoyance; users complain but tolerate it.
- **61–85 (Severe headache):** Significant wasted hours, direct financial loss, high stress, or risk of project failure.
- **86–100 (Existential / Critical):** Severe business disruption, compliance penalty, catastrophic data loss, or intense emotional despair.

### 3. Frequency (Weight: 0.08)
How often the target customer encounters the obstacle.
- **0–25 (Rare):** Once a year or once in a lifetime event.
- **26–60 (Intermittent):** Monthly or quarterly occurrence.
- **61–85 (Regular):** Weekly or multiple times per month.
- **86–100 (Continuous):** Daily, hourly, or embedded in constant core workflow.

### 4. Purchase Intent (Weight: 0.10)
Evidence that customers actively seek paid solutions, hire contractors, buy workarounds, or explicitly ask for paid tools.
- **0–25 (Zero intent):** Users expect everything free; hostility toward paid solutions.
- **26–60 (Passive intent):** Users ask for recommendations but primarily seek free alternatives or pirated scripts.
- **61–85 (Active intent):** Users explicitly asking "what tool can I buy?", comparing paid alternatives, or paying for imperfect workarounds.
- **86–100 (Urgent commercial intent):** High-budget commercial buyers actively seeking quotes, hiring consultants, or canceling unsatisfactory high-tier subscriptions.

### 5. Monetization Viability (Weight: 0.10)
Plausibility and margin potential of solving the problem with a digital product (template, toolkit, guide, workflow system).
- **0–25 (Unviable):** Problem requires ongoing manual services, heavy physical infrastructure, or sub-dollar commoditized pricing.
- **26–60 (Marginal):** Low perceived monetary value ($5–$15 impulse digital download only).
- **61–85 (Solid commercial):** Standard commercial digital product ($29–$199) delivering clear ROI or 10x time savings.
- **86–100 (High leverage):** Premium digital asset or system ($199–$999+) serving high-budget professionals or businesses with clear financial payback.

### 6. Competitive Landscape & Opportunity Gap (Weight: 0.07)
**Crucial Rule:** Competition is **not** automatically negative. The presence of competitors proves market demand. Attractiveness depends on whether existing solutions leave an exploitable gap.
- **0–25 (Impenetrable monopoly / commoditized red ocean):** Entrenched incumbents with zero churn, free open-source equivalents dominating, or race-to-the-bottom pricing.
- **26–60 (Crowded but mediocre):** Numerous competitors exist, but customers complain about high pricing, bloat, or poor usability.
- **61–85 (Validated market with clear gap):** Proven existing demand with documented churn, poor support, or complex enterprise tools that leave indie/SMB users unserved.
- **86–100 (Underserved greenfield / prime wedge):** Incumbents ignore a fast-growing use case; customers desperately hacking together clumsy spreadsheets or workarounds.

*Calculation convention:* Rate the **Opportunity Gap** directly on a 0–100 scale (where 100 represents the most attractive, exploitable competitive opening, and 0 represents an impenetrable lock-in).

### 7. Buildability (Weight: 0.07)
Ability to produce a truly valuable, reliable digital product using available capabilities, tools, and timelines.
- **0–25 (Unfeasible):** Requires custom hardware, massive real-time server infrastructure, or multi-year proprietary data scraping.
- **26–60 (Complex):** Requires intricate multi-platform software or extensive ongoing maintenance.
- **61–85 (Feasible):** Self-contained digital product, structured template system, automated workflow, or detailed technical guide.
- **86–100 (Optimal build velocity):** Highly structured, modular digital asset or workbook deliverable with pristine fidelity and immediate time-to-value.

### 8. Differentiation (Weight: 0.08)
Strength of the unique mechanism or proprietary angle compared to existing alternatives.
- **0–25 (Me-too):** Identical to generic blog posts or commodity templates.
- **26–60 (Slight edge):** Better design or cleaner interface, but fundamentally the same mechanism.
- **61–85 (Proprietary mechanism):** Unique structured methodology, novel workflow integration, or proprietary frameworks that solve root causes.
- **86–100 (Radical advantage):** Unmatched speed, proprietary workflow, or 10x simplification that competitors cannot easily duplicate.

### 9. Timing & Tailwinds (Weight: 0.06)
External catalysts (new regulations, platform updates, AI shifts, industry macro trends) accelerating problem relevance.
- **0–25 (Headwinds):** Declining industry, dying platform, or impending obsolescence.
- **26–60 (Neutral):** Stable, unchanging environment with no specific catalyst.
- **61–85 (Favorable tailwinds):** Recent tool updates, regulatory requirements, or workflow shifts driving users to adapt now.
- **86–100 (Inflection point):** Major platform rupture or sudden market shock creating urgent, immediate need for new tools.

### 10. Direct Distribution Feasibility (Weight: 0.06)
Ability to reach the target audience through direct organic channels, SEO, community distribution, or marketplace listings.
- **0–25 (Opaque):** Impossible to locate where buyers congregate; gated corporate hierarchies.
- **26–60 (Moderate friction):** Fragmented communities with strict anti-promotion moderation.
- **61–85 (Accessible):** Highly active dedicated subreddits, Discord servers, forums, and searchable keyword intent.
- **86–100 (Highly accessible):** Clear marketplace ecosystems, direct user search queries, and thriving open communities.

### 11. Creator / Influencer Fit (Weight: 0.07)
Suitability for organic creator collaboration, demonstrations, tutorial videos, and audience alignment.
- **0–25 (Unsuited):** Dull, abstract back-office code that cannot be demonstrated visually; zero creator niche.
- **26–60 (Weak demo):** Can be discussed in text/podcasts, but lacks visual before/after transformation.
- **61–85 (Strong demo potential):** Highly visual, dramatic before/after, excellent fit for tutorials, short-form breakdowns, and workflow tours.
- **86–100 (Viral demonstration magnet):** Compelling "watch this solve a 3-hour problem in 2 minutes" mechanic that creators eagerly showcase to build their own credibility.

### 12. Evidence Confidence (Weight: 0.07)
The epistemic quality, independence, freshness, and corroboration of the supporting evidence.
- **0–25 (Anecdotal / Speculative):** Single post or hearsay; no corroborating primary sources.
- **26–60 (Preliminary):** 2–3 community threads; plausible but lacks independent corroboration or commercial verification.
- **61–85 (Well-substantiated):** Multiple independent sources across Tiers 1–3; corroborated claims with clear provenance.
- **86–100 (Rigorously verified):** Cross-verified across primary data, customer discussions, independent reviews, and pricing benchmarks.

## 2. Weighted Overall Score Calculation

$$\text{Overall Score} = (0.12 \times \text{Demand}) + (0.12 \times \text{Pain}) + (0.08 \times \text{Frequency}) + (0.10 \times \text{PurchaseIntent}) + (0.10 \times \text{Monetization}) + (0.07 \times \text{OpportunityGap}) + (0.07 \times \text{Buildability}) + (0.08 \times \text{Differentiation}) + (0.06 \times \text{Timing}) + (0.06 \times \text{Distribution}) + (0.07 \times \text{CreatorFit}) + (0.07 \times \text{EvidenceConfidence})$$

Round to one decimal place.

---

## 3. Risk Adjustment & Confidence Gates

### Risk Adjustment Factor
If material risks are identified, the Opportunity Analyst must calculate an explicit **Risk Penalty** (deducting 5 to 20 points from the overall score) based on:
1. **Platform Dependency Risk:** Relying on an API or platform that could ban or break the product tomorrow (-5 to -10).
2. **Legal / Regulatory Risk:** Copyright, trademark, compliance, or liability hazards (-5 to -15).
3. **Execution Complexity Risk:** Risk of producing shallow or incomplete solutions (-5 to -10).

### Strict Confidence Gates
1. **The Speculation Trap:** Any opportunity with an overall score above 75, but an `EvidenceConfidence` score below 60, is strictly classified as **"High-Potential / Speculative Hypothesis"** and cannot proceed to Human Approval without further empirical validation.
2. **The Complaint Trap:** High pain or high demand with `PurchaseIntent` < 40 or `Monetization` < 40 must **never** be ranked #1. Whining is free; purchasing is not.
3. **The Vanity Trap:** High creator fit with low pain intensity (< 50) must be discarded. Products cannot survive solely on hype without utility.

---

## 4. Mandatory Reasoning Standard

For **every** dimension assigned a score below 30 or above 70, the Opportunity Analyst must provide a concise, evidence-backed justification citing specific source IDs from `memory/sources.csv`. Unexplained numbers are invalid.
